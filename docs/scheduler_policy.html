§ 3.4 — ARCHITECTURAL BACKDROP

Instance Disaggregation

The transformer layer is split at the attention/FFN boundary into two instances connected by an NVLink pipeline. This split is the prerequisite for everything in the scheduler policy that follows — KV cache permanence, fixed-shape handoff, and the Instance A internal joint GPU·PIM scheduling.

§ 6.2 · § 6.3 — STATIC RULES

Invariants & Dispatch DAG

The scheduler's policy reduces to a graph problem. Invariants (correctness + efficiency + resource) become edges in a per-μ-batch dependency DAG, and the dispatch decision collapses to picking a ready node.

Invariants (I1 — I5)

Whichever policy the scheduler adopts, these five rules cannot be violated. I1·I2 are correctness invariants (data dependency); I3 is an efficiency invariant (split O-proj is mathematically equivalent, but is strictly never split due to weight reuse · MFU · kernel launch penalties); I4·I5 are hardware resource constraints.

IDKindRule
I1 correctness prefill-attn(X) → dispatch only after QKV(X) completes (Q, K, V dependency)
I2 correctness decode-attn(X) → dispatch only after QKV(X) completes (PIM FSM start condition)
I3 efficiency O-proj(X) → dispatch only after both prefill-attn(X) ∧ decode-attn(X) complete. Although row-wise independence permits splitting, the penalties of W_O 2× streaming + MFU drop + 2× kernel launch keep it always a single GEMM. Aligned with the production mixed-batch standard pattern.
I4 resource At time t, the GPU resource runs only one GEMM / attention op (tensor core saturates; kernel concurrency ineffective).
I5 resource At time t, the PIM resource runs only one decode-attn op (SP-PIM occupies all 2048 channels). Not a head- or request-level constraint — within a single decode-attn op, multi-head · multi-request batching is free.

Dispatch DAG

DEFINITION Directed Acyclic Graph. Nodes are work units; an edge A → B denotes the precedence relation “B can be dispatched once A completes.” Acyclicity precludes deadlock and guarantees topological order. PULS encodes the §6.2 invariants as DAG edges, reducing the dispatch decision to a ready-node selection problem over a data structure.

Nodes. For each μ-batch X, four work nodes: QKV(X), prefill-attn(X), decode-attn(X), O-proj(X).

Edges. I1·I2·I3 become precedence edges directly:

EdgeSource invariant
QKV(X) → prefill-attn(X)I1
QKV(X) → decode-attn(X)I2
prefill-attn(X) → O-proj(X)I3
decode-attn(X) → O-proj(X)I3

Single μ-batch graph

Single μ-batch DAG. Diamond structure: QKV fans out to two attention nodes (GPU prefill / PIM decode), which fan in to O-proj. Edge labels mark the source invariant.

No inter-μ-batch edges — the basis of emergent interleaving

No explicit edges exist between distinct μ-batches. When resources (GPU·PIM) are available, arbitrary interleaving across μ-batches is possible — this is the graph-theoretic basis of look-ahead and back-fill. The scheduler does not encode a separate “lookahead policy”; the behavior emerges from greedy ready-set dispatch over a multi-μ-batch DAG (see §6.3 pseudo-code in Section 3, demonstrated in Section 5).

§ 6.3 — RUNTIME MECHANISM

Scheduler Usage

How the scheduler actually runs at each kernel-completion event: maintain the DAG over a 3-μ-batch window, refresh the ready set, and let two pick calls (one per resource) drive dispatch. The whole policy fits in nine lines.

Pseudo-code

The scheduler maintains a DAG over the in-flight μ-batch window {Mi-1, Mi, Mi+1}, and at every kernel-completion event dispatches ready work from the two queues (GPU·PIM).

on event(kernel K of μ-batch X completes):
    update DAG: mark node K(X) as done
    refresh ready set: all unexecuted nodes whose precedences are done

    GPU_next = pick(ready GPU jobs,
                    priority: O-proj > prefill-attn > QKV,
                    tie-break: oldest μ-batch first)
    PIM_next = pick(ready PIM jobs,
                    tie-break: oldest μ-batch first)

    if GPU idle: dispatch GPU_next
    if PIM idle: dispatch PIM_next

pick — definition

PRIORITY-QUEUE DEQUEUE A function with arguments (candidate set, priority rule, tie-break rule). Behavior: among candidates, narrow down to nodes belonging to the top-priority class of the priority rule, then pick a single node within that subset via the tie-break rule. Returns null if the candidate set is empty (in which case the resource sits idle).

GPU priority rationale — O-proj > prefill-attn > QKV

Why O-proj first. Delaying O-proj is a delay of the current μ-batch's completion and thus directly impacts the critical path. Once O-proj is ready (I3 satisfied), nothing else on the queue should jump ahead of it.

Why QKV last. QKV is the next μ-batch's enabling work, not the current one's critical path. It is prioritized only when the higher-priority classes are empty — that is, only when there is a risk of PIM going idle (no current-μ-batch attention or projection work left to do).

This ordering, applied as a simple priority dequeue, is what makes GPU back-fill emerge: when O-proj is blocked (waiting on PIM for I3) and prefill-attn is also not ready (its μ-batch's QKV not yet done), the dequeue naturally falls through to QKV of the next μ-batch.

Sync guarantee

Since I3 is auto-enforced as a DAG edge, there is no risk of missing synchronization. The DAG itself encodes the precondition; a node simply does not enter the ready set until its precedences are done.

Owing to PIM FSM determinism (§3.1), the PIM completion time is computed at the moment PIM is dispatched. The scheduler therefore knows in advance when O-proj will become ready, and the next GPU dispatch can be pre-scheduled to that exact moment.

Natural emergence of look-ahead / back-fill

Since I2 is loose — “any time after QKV completes” — at moments when PIM is idle, decode-attn of another μ-batch can be started regardless of which μ-batch the GPU is currently working on. This emerges automatically from greedy dispatch without any separate policy specification.

Symmetrically, when GPU's higher-priority classes are exhausted but PIM is still busy, GPU falls through to the next μ-batch's QKV (back-fill). Both behaviors — look-ahead on PIM and back-fill on GPU — are properties of the same priority dequeue applied to a multi-μ-batch DAG. See the worked trace in Section 5 for a concrete demonstration.

§ 6.4 — RUNTIME ADAPTATION

Adaptive Admission

Per-iteration adjustment of the μ-batch composition based on measured GPU/PIM idle fractions. Hooked on top of the chunked-prefill primitive (§5.5) as dynamic adjustment of chunk size · decode batch.

The scheduler dynamically adjusts the per-μ-batch composition decision on a per-iteration basis. The GPU/PIM idle fractions of the previous iteration are measured to regulate the next μ-batch's admission.

Decision Rule — Two Layers

The essential frame of admission control:

  • Layer 1 — μ-batch composition (on top of the chunked-prefill + mixed-batching primitive): decides the prefill-chunk vs decode token mix and N. The determining factor of TTFT / TBT SLO condenses to this layer.
  • Layer 2 — DAG dispatch (§6.3, Section 3): Automatic ready-node selection on top of the Layer 1 result. Since processing is serial, it cancels with admission variables — adaptive degrees of freedom concentrate in Layer 1.

Primary objective — balance the two instances of the inter-instance pipeline cycle max(A_cycle, B_cycle) so that both are fully utilized.

Secondary objective — balance the GPU·PIM double-buffering inside Instance A (§5.6).

Figure. Event-driven dispatch trace of PULS scheduler over a 3-μ-batch in-flight window {Mi−1, Mi, Mi+1} (here labeled P/M/N), maintained as a dependency DAG.
  • Mechanism At each kernel-completion event Ti, ready nodes (precedence satisfied per I1·I2·I3) are picked greedily — priority O-proj > prefill-attn > QKV; tie-break: oldest μ-batch first. Both emergent behaviors below arise from this single priority dequeue — no explicit lookahead policy is encoded.
  • GPU back-fill (Init, T3) While PIM is busy and the current μ-batch's O-proj is I3-blocked, GPU falls through priority to the next μ-batch's QKV.
  • PIM look-ahead (T1, T4) The next μ-batch's decode-attn is I2-ready the moment its QKV completes during the back-fill window; PIM dispatches it at the very PIM-completion trigger.
  • P at Init = tail state P's prefill-attn(X) and earlier GPU stages already completed; only decode-attn(G,H) remains on PIM.
  • Assumption Balanced admission steady state (cycle-balanced per §6.4: t_PIM(decode-attn) ≈ sum of GPU stages per μ-batch). The trace shape is the ctx-independent attractor under this balance — chunked prefill (§5.5) lets admission scale chunk granularity to maintain it across any ctx within TBT SLO. The figure ceases to apply only at very long ctx where balance is infeasible (§6.6 "A-bound natural transition") — a system-level limit of multi-request schedulers, not PULS-specific.
